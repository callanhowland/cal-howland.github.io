Cal Howland

PhD, Rutgers University

I work at the intersection of philosophy of language, social philosophy, and cognitive science. My research asks how words do social work: how names, slurs, and misgendering shape who we take each other to be, and how formal semantics and pragmatics can make those harms precise.

Alongside this, I run experiments on language and spatial cognition in the Priors and Memory (PRIME) Lab at Rutgers.

Carving Dynamicness at its Joints

Draft

Abstract

[Placeholder — replace with your abstract.] Rothschild and Yalcin characterize static semantic systems by idempotence and commutativity; van Benthem characterizes them by eliminativity and distributivity. It is natural to read the former as a conservative extension of the latter. I argue that the two criteria diverge within van Benthem's own domain, and I relate Rothschild and Yalcin's representation theorem to Cayley's theorem.
